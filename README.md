# VendorRunway: Retail Media Network for Convenience Stores (Case Study)

> A platform concept connecting FMCG brands and distributors with convenience-store franchise owners, so brands can run in-store promotions and stores earn from their shelf and counter space.

**Status:** Working demo app plus investor/partner pitch deck · **Role:** Founder, product designer & builder · **Code:** private

---

## The idea

Big retailers earn significant revenue from "retail media": brands paying for visibility in-store. Independent convenience-store owners have the same valuable shelf space and foot traffic, but no simple way to sell it. FMCG vendors and distributors, meanwhile, have no easy channel to reach hundreds of small stores with targeted promotions.

VendorRunway connects the two.

## What I built

A **three-sided demo application**, one surface per user type:

| Surface | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Vendor dashboard** | Create promotions, choose stores, track leads and results |
| **Retailer dashboard** | See available promotions, accept the right ones, track earnings |
| **Admin** | Analytics and oversight across the whole network |

Plus **Promotion details**, and **Messages & Leads** for vendor-retailer communication.

I also designed a **pitch deck** covering the problem, the product (with the 5 core screens) and the business model.

## Stack and approach

- **Next.js 14, Tailwind CSS, Supabase**
- **Demo mode first:** the full app runs on realistic mock data, so it could be shown to store owners and vendors before any backend existed, then switched to live Supabase
- **Row Level Security designed from day one**, so vendors, retailers and admins only ever see their own data

## Why it matters

The project comes from real contact with convenience-store owners in the US, and it applies what I learned from 12+ years in B2B distribution: small retailers and suppliers both want more sales, but lack a simple, trusted channel to work together.
